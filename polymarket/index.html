It priced each challenger as P(chal ends above today’s leader) = Φ(ln(capchal/caplead) / σ√T) and then defined the leader’s own leg as 100 − Σ(challengers) − small legs. These books do not resolve on “did you pass the leader”; they resolve on “are you the largest”. The two differ because the passing events overlap — one bad quarter for the leader pushes several challengers past at once — so Σ(challengers) is a union bound that overstates P(someone passes), and the entire error landed in the residual. Measured bias: the leader leg read 6.3 pts low in August and 24.5 pts low in December, and the challengers carried an equal and opposite error (December: Apple +9.2, Alphabet +10.9, Microsoft +3.8, Amazon +0.6 — summing to exactly the leader’s 24.5), which is where the page’s long-running “+31.7 on NVIDIA-December” came from. The replacement keeps the identical diffusion and the identical published σ, and just does the integral. Each name gets idiosyncratic log-vol σ/√2, so every pair’s log-ratio still carries σrel = 2%/day and any common market factor cancels (it must — a ranking is scale-invariant). The touch model

P(30Y prints ≥6.00% on a daily close by Dec 31) uses the exact drifted first-passage probability — Φ((μT−a)/σ√T) + e^(2μa/σ²)·Φ((−a−μT)/σ√T) — with a Broadie–Glasserman–Kou barrier shift (+0.5826·σ√Δt) because the ladder resolves on one daily print, not the intraday path. That distinction is not pedantry: in Oct 2023 the 10-year famously “hit 5%” intraday (5.02%) while the daily par yield peaked at 4.98% — the most celebrated touch of the last cycle would have resolved NO on these markets’ terms. Re-derived Aug 10 on a live curve for the first time since Jul 30: a = 75 bp from the 5.25% close, T = 101 trading days, σ = 4.0 bp/day (measured: 20d 4.20, 40d 3.80, 60d 3.89, full-2026 3.76). The two models, and the mixture

Martingale: zero-drift random walk on the 10-year, σ = 4.4 bp/day (measured 4.26–4.60 across windows), same first-passage machinery and BGK shift. AR(1): fitted on 2026, φ = 0.98089, attractor 4.549%, half-life 35.9 trading days. The fair is their equal-weight mixture, and the honest reason is that neither survives on its own. The AR(1) is probably Dickey–Fuller small-sample bias with a story attached: simulating true random walks at n = 151 produces a φ̂ at or below 0.98089 52.8% of the time, and an ADF test has only ~10% power against it here — so “the series mean-reverts” is not something this data can establish. The martingale, meanwhile, ignores that yields have looked mean-reverting to everyone pricing these books all year. The mixture lands within a point of the market on both live rungs (35.0 vs 34.00, 13.8 vs 14.15), which is what an efficient market facing genuine model uncertainty should look like. We are not claiming the mixture is right. We are claiming the market is not obviously wrong, which is what this tab spent ten days asserting.

The smile, and what a constant σ does to it

Inverting each rung to the σ that makes it fair at zero drift gives 1.94 / 3.11 / 3.38 / 4.48 / 4.97 / 5.87 bp per day across the 4.8 → 6.00 strikes — a clean monotone smile. Against a rising smile, any constant-σ model must call the near rungs cheap and the far rungs rich. This tab reported exactly that pattern for ten days and read it as a finding; it was the assumption looking at itself.
